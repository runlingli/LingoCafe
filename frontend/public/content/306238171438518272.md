Basic negation in French wraps the conjugated verb in **ne...pas**. In writing and formal speech, both parts are required. In everyday spoken French, the *ne* is frequently dropped — you will hear *Je mange pas* constantly — but write both.

## The Basic Pattern

> *Je **ne** mange **pas**.* — I don't eat / I'm not eating.
> *Elle **ne** comprend **pas**.* — She doesn't understand.
> *Nous **ne** savons **pas**.* — We don't know.

The *ne* becomes *n'* before a vowel or silent *h*:

> *Je **n'**aime **pas** ça.* — I don't like that.
> *Il **n'**habite **pas** ici.* — He doesn't live here.

## Negation in Compound Tenses

In compound tenses, **ne...pas surround the auxiliary**, not the past participle:

> ✓ *Je **n'**ai **pas** mangé.* — I didn't eat.
> ✗ ~~*Je n'ai mangé pas.*~~

> ✓ *Elle **n'**est **pas** venue.* — She didn't come.
> ✓ *Nous **n'**avons **pas** pu.* — We weren't able to.

This is one of the most common written errors, even among advanced learners.

## Beyond Ne...Pas

French has a rich family of negative structures:

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| *ne...jamais* | never | *Je ne fume jamais.* |
| *ne...rien* | nothing | *Il ne dit rien.* |
| *ne...personne* | nobody | *Elle ne voit personne.* |
| *ne...plus* | no longer | *Nous n'habitons plus là.* |
| *ne...que* | only | *Il ne boit que de l'eau.* |
| *ne...ni...ni* | neither...nor | *Je n'aime ni le café ni le thé.* |
| *ne...nulle part* | nowhere | *Je ne vais nulle part.* |
| *ne...aucun(e)* | not any | *Il n'a aucune idée.* |

Note: *rien*, *personne*, and *aucun* can also appear as subjects — in that case, they come before the verb:

> *Rien ne m'étonne.* — Nothing surprises me.
> *Personne n'est venu.* — Nobody came.

## Articles After Negation

After *ne...pas* (and most negatives), **indefinite and partitive articles change to *de***:

| Affirmative | Negative |
|-------------|---------|
| *Je veux **du** café.* | *Je ne veux **pas de** café.* |
| *J'ai **des** amis.* | *Je n'ai **pas d'**amis.* |
| *Elle mange **une** pomme.* | *Elle ne mange **pas de** pomme.* |

**Exception**: after *être*, the article does **not** change:

> *C'est **un** problème. → Ce n'est **pas un** problème.*
> *C'est **du** travail. → Ce n'est **pas du** travail.*
