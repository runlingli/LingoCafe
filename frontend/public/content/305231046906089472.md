French nouns carry **grammatical gender** — every noun is either masculine (*le, un*) or feminine (*la, une*) — and this gender ripples through the entire sentence, affecting articles, adjectives, and pronouns. Unfortunately, gender cannot be predicted reliably from meaning alone, so learners must treat it as part of the noun itself.

> Always learn nouns with their article: not *livre* but **le livre**.

## Reliable Endings

While no rule is absolute, certain endings give strong signals:

### Usually Feminine

| Ending | Examples |
|--------|---------|
| -tion, -sion | *la nation*, *la décision* |
| -ure | *la culture*, *la nature* |
| -ette | *la fourchette*, *la cigarette* |
| -ance, -ence | *la chance*, *la science* |
| -té | *la liberté*, *la beauté* |
| -ière | *la frontière*, *la rivière* |

### Usually Masculine

| Ending | Examples |
|--------|---------|
| -age | *le voyage*, *le fromage* |
| -ment | *le gouvernement*, *le sentiment* |
| -isme | *le tourisme*, *le réalisme* |
| -eau | *le gâteau*, *le chapeau* |
| -eur (from verb) | *le bonheur*, *le malheur* |

## Tricky Categories

**Borrowed words** from English tend to be masculine by default: *le smartphone*, *le week-end*, *le parking*.

**Compound nouns** generally take the gender of the primary noun: *le porte-monnaie* (purse, masculine).

## Gender Changes Meaning

Some words exist in both genders with different meanings:

| Masculine | Feminine |
|-----------|----------|
| *le livre* (book) | *la livre* (pound) |
| *le tour* (tour, trick) | *la tour* (tower) |
| *le poste* (job, post) | *la poste* (post office) |
| *le mémoire* (dissertation) | *la mémoire* (memory) |

## Study Strategy

Use colour-coded flashcards — one colour for masculine, another for feminine. Over time, patterns crystallise and guessing becomes more reliable. But never entirely safe: when in doubt, check.
